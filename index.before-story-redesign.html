The 30-second version

If you only read one thing

The whole page in about 150 words.

Snapp Group is twelve businesses under one name. When I joined the central team, nobody could clearly say what that team was for. So before designing anything, I asked people what they believed Central was founded to do. Their answers became Color and Canvas: the group is the canvas, the ventures are the colors, and Central is the thread that connects them. Next I led an EVP built from three sources: leadership interviews, 124 people from across the ventures and 1,144 people who had touched Snapp Group. I gave that promise a home in one central career page for all twelve ventures, and a voice on LinkedIn. I took it to three job fairs, where our rank among 50 companies went from fifth to third to second. Then I ran a five-week product bootcamp. 21 of its 33 attendees asked to join us. We hired four, and all four have since been promoted.

Act 1 of 6 · Identity

Color and Canvas: finding out who we were

Before any campaign, the group needed an answer to a simple question. What is the central team for?

Tension

The central team's identity was unclear, and that hurt decisions, especially in branding. Was Central a supporter, the leader and decision-maker, or just another venture? Projects followed global best practice but did not fit the group's own identity.

What I did

My first move was to ask. I asked colleagues and stakeholders what they believed Central's mission was, and what intent it had been founded on.

Result

A concept the group could use: Color and Canvas. Snapp Group is the canvas. Ventures are the colors: brands, businesses, cultures. Central is the thread that supports and connects them.

Act 2 of 6 · Employer value proposition

One promise for twelve ventures

Ventures that share the Snapp name also share each other's employer reputation. What one says, the others inherit.

Tension

Job posters had different designs and messages. Slogans were scattered, with different perks and promises that weakened the brand. Hiring was traditional, and platforms carried the Snapp name without being used well. ADD a number for the old state

What I did

I led the group-wide EVP from research to launch: survey design, insight analysis, messaging, bilingual content and the activation plan.

Result

Four pillars, twelve sub-pillars and one line: “Individually bold. Together, Masterpiece!”

“Each Impressionist painting has its own world at first glance, but a shared spirit connects them. This mirrors Snapp Group's story: each operational unit has its own character, yet all operate with a shared vision, expressing the same spirit in their own way.”

    Act 4 of 6 · LinkedIn

    A voice with a plan behind it

    The promise needed to be heard every week, not only on launch day.

    Tension

    Content had no structured, unified strategy and no measurable goals.

    What I did

    I run the employer-brand content strategy. I set quarterly goals, write the LinkedIn content for every event, and run targeted campaigns.

    Result

    One EVP-aligned voice for the group, tracked every quarter against goals for activity, engagement and followers.

    The quarterly loop

    How I run it

    1. Set the quarter's goals

      Three measures, set at the start of every quarter.

      ActivityEngagementFollowers

    2. Write every event

      Each fair, talk and program gets its own LinkedIn story, written by me.

    3. Run targeted campaigns

      Campaigns aimed at the audiences we need to reach.

    4. Review against goals

      Results feed the next quarter's plan.

    Repeat every quarter

    One at the University of Tehran and two at Sharif University of Technology. All 12 ventures came to each, each with its own openings.

    What I did

    I managed the contact with every venture, the slogan, the booth design and the activities.
